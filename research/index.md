## Self-representation during motor control

To effectively move your muscles, your brain needs to constantly monitor the state of the body, and it needs to know something about the body's physics. We are interested in how people (and other organisms!) sense and represent the state of their body. Past research has focused on how we may exploit regularities introduced by the body's biomechanics to link specific motor actions to their sensory consequences.

## Conscious awareness of sensorimotor contingencies (i.e., the sense of agency)

Our brain processes self-caused sensations differently from how it processes externally caused sensations, even if we're not consciously aware we did anything at all. Sometimes though, we sense an event and feel "Hey, I did that!" That's the _sense of agency_, and we're interested in (a) when and why we feel it to begin with and (b) what makes this _conscious_ self-recognition distinct from other types of action-outcome processing in the brain.

## Influences of interoceptive sensory signals on exteroceptive perception

We're all familiar with our _exteroceptive_ senses: sight, hearing, smell, taste, and touch. Did you know there are also sensory organs inside the body? These _interoceptive senses_ and responsible for keeping the brain informed about what's going in in the body's other organs (e.g.. the heart, lungs, and gut). We're interested in how interoceptive senses alter brain processes, and how those alterations affect our subjective experiences. A nice illustration: We've done some neat studies where participants may perceive the same physical stimulus (e.g., an image) totally differently depending on their heartbeat!
